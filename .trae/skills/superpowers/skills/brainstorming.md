# Brainstorming Skill

A structured approach to generating and exploring ideas through systematic brainstorming techniques.

## When to Use

- User needs to generate ideas for a project or problem
- Exploring multiple solutions or approaches
- Need creative thinking or out-of-the-box ideas
- Analyzing a problem from different angles

## Brainstorming Framework

### 1. Problem Definition

Start by clearly defining the problem or challenge:

- What is the core issue?
- What constraints exist?
- What are the success criteria?

### 2. Idea Generation Phase

Generate ideas without judgment:

- Quantity over quality initially
- Build on others' ideas
- Encourage wild ideas
- Defer evaluation

### 3. Idea Exploration

For each idea, explore:

- **What**: What does this idea do?
- **Why**: Why would this work?
- **Risks**: What could go wrong?
- **Alternatives**: What variations exist?

### 4. Synthesis

Combine and refine ideas:

- Find common themes
- Merge complementary ideas
- Prioritize by impact and feasibility

## Techniques

### SCAMPER
- **S**ubstitute: What else?
- **C**ombine: How can we combine ideas?
- **A**dapt: What can we adapt?
- **M**odify: What can we modify?
- **P**ut to other use: What else can we use this for?
- **E**liminate: What can we remove?
- **R**everse: What if we do the opposite?

### Six Thinking Hats
- **White**: Facts and information
- **Red**: Emotions and feelings
- **Black**: Critical judgment
- **Yellow**: Optimism and benefits
- **Green**: Creative alternatives
- **Blue**: Process control

### Reverse Brainstorming
1. How to make the problem worse?
2. How to solve the inverse problem?
3. Transform those solutions back

- [ ] Action item 2

## Best Practices

1. Start with a clear problem statement
2. Set a time box for idea generation
3. Never reject ideas during generation
4. Document everything
5. Review and refine after a break
